Best Meal Planning Apps in 2026: Compare Features, Grocery Lists & Pricing

Written by Lightning Meal Plans Team•Updated August 13, 2026
📱

The best meal planning app is not necessarily the one with the longest feature list. It is the one that removes the biggest source of friction in your own week: choosing dinners, saving recipes, building a grocery list, or keeping everyone at home on the same page.

We compared six apps that cover the most common approaches: guided meal suggestions, recipe-first planning, shared grocery lists, and AI-assisted planning. This article is published by Lightning Meal Plans, so we have aimed to be especially clear about where another app may be the better fit.

At a glance: the best app for each kind of planner

  • Lightning Meal Plans: Best for people who want personalized suggestions, imported recipes, serving-size changes, and a combined grocery list in one workflow.
  • Mealime: Best for guided plans built around its recipe collection and dietary preferences.
  • Paprika: Best for people building a personal recipe collection who prefer a one-time purchase.
  • Plan to Eat: Best for hands-on planners who want to collect recipes and schedule them on a calendar.
  • AnyList: Best for households that care most about shared lists, recipe organization, and a shared calendar.
  • Samsung Food: Best for people who want a free starting point with recipe saving, planning, and shopping-list collaboration.

What each app does best

Lightning Meal Plans

Best for: People who want the app to do more of the deciding, then carry the plan through recipe import, serving-size changes, and one combined shopping list. It is a good fit when allergies, dislikes, dietary preferences, or a busy schedule need to shape the suggestions.

Consider another option if: You only need a simple shared grocery list or you strongly prefer a one-time purchase.

Mealime

Best for: People who like choosing from a curated set of recipes and want help assembling a plan that works with their dietary preferences. Its official guide describes an auto-builder, grocery lists that combine quantities and sort by department, and web-recipe import.

Consider another option if: You mainly want to organize a large existing recipe collection rather than start with a guided meal-plan builder.

Paprika

Best for: Recipe collectors who want a capable recipe manager, web clipping, meal planning, pantry tracking, and aisle-sorted grocery lists without a recurring subscription. Paprika is sold separately by platform, so check the total cost if you use several devices.

Consider another option if: You want the app to suggest a weekly plan for you rather than build the plan from recipes you save.

Plan to Eat

Best for: Deliberate planners who want to save recipes from the web, drag them onto a calendar, and automatically build an aisle-sorted shopping list. Its current public price is $5.95 per month or $49 per year after a 14-day trial.

Consider another option if: You want a more suggestion-led experience instead of selecting and scheduling your own recipes.

AnyList

Best for: Households that want collaboration first: shared lists, shared recipes, and a shared meal-planning calendar. AnyList Complete also imports recipes from the web, combines similar ingredients, and can generate a shopping list from a selected date range.

Consider another option if: You want an app to discover or generate new meal ideas beyond the recipes you have already saved.

Samsung Food

Best for: People who want to start with a free recipe-saving, planning, and list-sharing tool. Samsung Food can import recipes, add plans to a shopping list, and sort the list by aisle or recipe. Food+ adds tailored seven-day plans and other premium tools.

Consider another option if: You want premium plan suggestions but prefer a simpler, lower-cost subscription.

How we handled this comparison: We used each company’s own feature and pricing documentation, not app-store reviews or third-party roundups. Products change often, so check the linked official pages before purchasing.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best free meal planning app?

Mealime and Samsung Food both offer free tiers. The better choice depends on whether you prefer Mealime’s guided recipe approach or Samsung Food’s recipe-saving and sharing workflow. AnyList is also free for core list features, though its recipe-import and meal-planning tools require AnyList Complete.

What is the best meal planning app with a grocery list?

All six apps in this comparison can turn recipes or a meal plan into a grocery list. Choose based on the rest of the workflow: Lightning Meal Plans for personalized planning, Mealime for curated guidance, Paprika or Plan to Eat for recipe-first planning, AnyList for shared household lists, and Samsung Food for a free collaborative starting point.

What is the best app for saving recipes and planning meals?

Paprika, Plan to Eat, AnyList, and Samsung Food are especially strong choices when saving recipes is the starting point. Lightning Meal Plans and Mealime are better suited to people who also want assistance deciding what to cook.

The bottom line

A meal planning app should make the next grocery trip and next dinner easier, not add another system to maintain. Start with the part you dislike most: finding meals, collecting recipes, keeping a household list current, or doing the shopping-list math. That points to the right app faster than a generic feature count.
