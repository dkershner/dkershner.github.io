Plant-Based Meal Prep: Complete Vegan Weekly Plan with Protein

Written by Lightning Meal Plans Team • Published August 3, 2026
🌱

The hardest part about eating plant-based isn't giving up meat. It's figuring out how to feel full and satisfied without defaulting to pasta and bread at every meal. And then there's the protein question everyone asks: "But where do you get your protein?"

Here's the reality: plant-based eating can be incredibly satisfying, nutrient-dense, and protein-rich—if you know how to plan meals properly. The problem is that most people wing it, end up hungry, and give up within a month.

The Protein Problem (And Why It's Easier Than You Think)

Yes, you need protein. Most adults need 50-100g per day depending on activity level and body size. Getting that from plants is absolutely doable—you just have to be slightly more intentional than someone eating chicken breasts.

High-Protein Plant Foods (per 100g cooked)

  • Tempeh: 19g protein
  • Seitan: 25g protein
  • Edamame: 11g protein
  • Lentils: 9g protein
  • Chickpeas: 9g protein
  • Black beans: 9g protein
  • Tofu (firm): 8g protein
  • Quinoa: 4g protein
  • Hemp seeds: 32g protein (per 100g dry)
  • Peanut butter: 25g protein (per 100g)

The trick isn't just eating these foods. It's structuring your meals so every single one has a strong protein source at the center, not as an afterthought.

The Foundation: Plant Proteins to Stock

Before planning meals, make sure you have these staples on hand:

Pantry

  • Dried or canned lentils (red, green, brown)
  • Canned black beans, chickpeas, kidney beans
  • Quinoa
  • Hemp seeds, chia seeds
  • Peanut butter or almond butter
  • Nutritional yeast (adds cheesy flavor + B12)

Fridge/Freezer

  • Firm or extra-firm tofu
  • Tempeh
  • Edamame (frozen)
  • Plant-based milk (soy has the most protein)

Optional But Helpful

  • Seitan (if you can find it or make it)
  • TVP (textured vegetable protein) for ground meat replacement
  • Plant-based protein powder for smoothies

Sample Week: High-Protein Plant-Based Meals

Here's what a realistic week of satisfying, protein-packed plant-based meals looks like:

Monday

Breakfast: Overnight oats (oats + soy milk + chia seeds + peanut butter + banana) — ~15g protein
Lunch: Chickpea salad sandwich (mashed chickpeas + vegan mayo + celery + spices on whole grain bread) — ~18g protein
Dinner: Tofu stir-fry (firm tofu + broccoli + bell peppers + soy sauce + sesame oil over rice) — ~20g protein

Tuesday

Breakfast: Tofu scramble (crumbled tofu + nutritional yeast + turmeric + vegetables + toast) — ~18g protein
Lunch: Lentil soup with whole grain crackers — ~16g protein
Dinner: Black bean tacos (black beans + taco seasoning + corn tortillas + avocado + salsa) — ~20g protein

Wednesday

Breakfast: Smoothie (soy milk + banana + spinach + peanut butter + protein powder) — ~25g protein
Lunch: Leftover tofu stir-fry — ~20g protein
Dinner: Tempeh Buddha bowl (marinated tempeh + quinoa + roasted sweet potato + tahini dressing) — ~25g protein

Thursday

Breakfast: Avocado toast on whole grain bread with hemp seeds — ~12g protein
Lunch: Chickpea pasta with marinara sauce and nutritional yeast — ~22g protein
Dinner: Lentil curry (red lentils + coconut milk + curry spices + vegetables over rice) — ~18g protein

Friday

Breakfast: Peanut butter banana oatmeal — ~14g protein
Lunch: Leftover lentil curry — ~18g protein
Dinner: BBQ tempeh sandwich (marinated tempeh + BBQ sauce + coleslaw + whole grain bun) with baked beans — ~28g protein

Weekend

Breakfast: Tofu scramble or pancakes made with chickpea flour
Lunch: Big salad with chickpeas, sunflower seeds, and tahini dressing
Dinner: Homemade veggie burgers (black bean or lentil-based) with sweet potato fries

Daily Protein Average: 70-90g, well above the minimum requirement for most adults.

The "Always Hungry" Problem

A lot of people try plant-based eating and feel hungry all the time. This usually happens for three reasons:

1. Not Enough Protein Per Meal

If your lunch is a salad with some chickpeas sprinkled on top, you've got maybe 5-7g of protein. That's not enough. You need 20-25g per meal to feel satisfied.

Fix: Make protein the star. A chickpea salad should have a full can of chickpeas (45g protein), not a tablespoon.

2. Not Enough Healthy Fats

Fat keeps you full. Plant-based diets can accidentally become too low-fat if you're just eating vegetables and grains.

Fix: Add nuts, seeds, avocado, tahini, or nut butter to every meal.

3. Too Many Refined Carbs

White pasta, white rice, and white bread digest quickly, spiking blood sugar and leaving you hungry soon after.

Fix: Choose whole grains (brown rice, quinoa, whole wheat pasta) and pair them with protein and fat.

Meal Prep Strategies for Plant-Based Eating

Strategy 1: Batch Cook Your Proteins

Cook large batches of lentils, chickpeas, and grains on Sunday. Store separately in containers. During the week, combine them in different ways:

  • Monday: Lentils + curry spices + coconut milk = lentil curry
  • Wednesday: Same lentils + marinara sauce + pasta = lentil bolognese
  • Friday: Same lentils + taco seasoning + tortillas = lentil tacos

You cooked lentils once, but you have three completely different meals.

Strategy 2: Pre-Marinate Tofu and Tempeh

Tofu and tempeh absorb flavor from marinades. Prep 3-4 blocks at once with different marinades:

  • Teriyaki tofu
  • BBQ tempeh
  • Curry-spiced tofu
  • Sesame-ginger tempeh

Store in fridge for up to 5 days or freeze. When you need dinner, just pan-fry or bake.

Strategy 3: Build Flexible Bowls

Prep components separately, assemble differently each day:

  • Base: Rice, quinoa, or greens
  • Protein: Tofu, tempeh, or legumes
  • Vegetables: Roasted or raw
  • Sauce: Tahini, peanut, teriyaki, or cashew cream

Mix and match throughout the week for variety without cooking from scratch every night.

Common Plant-Based Meal Planning Mistakes

Mistake 1: Relying Too Heavily on Processed Meat Substitutes

Beyond Burgers and Impossible meats are fine occasionally, but they're expensive and highly processed. Whole food plant proteins (beans, lentils, tofu) are cheaper and healthier.

Mistake 2: Forgetting About Iron

Plant-based iron (non-heme iron) is less bioavailable than meat-based iron. You need more of it, and pairing it with vitamin C helps absorption.

Good combinations:

  • Lentils + tomatoes (vitamin C)
  • Spinach salad + lemon juice
  • Chickpeas + bell peppers

Mistake 3: Not Planning for B12

B12 doesn't naturally occur in plant foods. You need a supplement or fortified foods (nutritional yeast, plant milk, cereals). This isn't optional—B12 deficiency causes serious problems.

Mistake 4: Eating the Same Thing Every Day

If you find three plant-based meals you like and eat them on repeat forever, you'll get bored and quit. Variety matters for both nutrition and mental sustainability.

Budget-Friendly Plant-Based Eating

Contrary to popular belief, plant-based eating can be very affordable. Beans, lentils, rice, and oats are some of the cheapest foods per calorie.

Cost Comparison (Approximate per serving of protein)

  • Dried lentils: $0.30
  • Canned chickpeas: $0.50
  • Tofu: $0.75
  • Tempeh: $1.00
  • Chicken breast: $1.50-2.00
  • Beyond Burger: $2.50-3.00

If you stick to whole food plant proteins, you'll likely spend less on groceries than when buying meat. It's the specialty vegan products (cheese, ice cream, fake meat) that get expensive.

What About Dining Out?

Most restaurants now have at least one or two plant-based options. But meal planning helps here too—if you know what you're eating at home, you can budget for occasional restaurant meals without feeling like you're constantly trying to figure out what's vegan on a menu.

Transitioning to Plant-Based (If You're Not Already)

If you're coming from a standard diet, going fully plant-based overnight can feel overwhelming. A gradual approach works better for most people:

Week 1-2: Meatless Mondays

Plan one fully plant-based dinner per week. Get comfortable with cooking tofu, lentils, or beans.

Week 3-4: Meatless Weekdays

Expand to all weekday dinners plant-based. Weekends can still include meat if you want.

Month 2: Add Plant-Based Lunches

Start meal prepping plant-based lunches using the strategies above.

Month 3: Fully Plant-Based (If That's Your Goal)

By now you have a rotation of meals you like, you know how to get enough protein, and it doesn't feel restrictive.

Or maybe you stay at 80-90% plant-based. That's fine too. The point is having a system that works for you, not following someone else's rules.
